
The PlantDoc error analysis revealed that the performance degradation was not uniformly distributed across classes. Several categories obtained an F1-score of zero, including Potato Early blight, Potato Late blight, Soybean healthy, Strawberry healthy, Tomato Bacterial spot, Tomato Leaf Mold, Tomato mosaic virus, Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus, and Tomato healthy. This indicates that the model failed to correctly recognize a substantial subset of external real-world classes, despite achieving high validation accuracy on PlantVillage.

The confusion analysis showed that many PlantDoc samples were incorrectly assigned to a small number of visually dominant classes, particularly Tomato Late blight and Strawberry Leaf scorch. For example, 87.5% of Potato Late blight images were classified as Tomato Late blight, while 87.5% of Strawberry healthy images were classified as Strawberry Leaf scorch. Similarly, 66.7% of Tomato Early blight images were classified as Tomato Late blight. These errors suggest that the model relied heavily on lesion texture, color patterns, and local necrotic regions rather than learning crop-specific and context-robust representations.

Most errors occurred between classes with visually similar symptoms, such as blight-like lesions, leaf spots, discoloration, or necrotic regions. However, several cross-crop confusions were also observed, such as Potato Late blight being predicted as Tomato Late blight. This confirms that a model trained only on controlled PlantVillage images may learn disease-specific visual shortcuts that do not generalize well to field-like images.

Overall, these findings demonstrate that high performance on PlantVillage alone is not sufficient to guarantee real-world reliability. The strong performance drop on PlantDoc highlights the need for cross-dataset validation, domain adaptation, stronger data augmentation, and fine-tuning with realistic field images before deploying plant disease recognition systems in practical agricultural environments.
